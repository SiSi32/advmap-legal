He is the data controller within the meaning of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). For any question or request about your data: advmap@sisilab.app.

2. What we collect

Your account: the email address passed on by Google or Apple when you sign in (if you use Apple's "Hide My Email", we only receive a relay address) — or, for the few accounts created by the ADVMAP team, the address chosen when they were created —, an internal identifier, your username, profile picture, bio, motorcycle details, language, theme and, if you choose one, your area ("My area"). Your username is picked at random when you sign up ("rider-…") and your profile has no picture: we take neither the name nor the picture of your Google or Apple account. You change them in the Profile tab: the pencil next to your username, or a tap on your picture.

What you publish: your GPX tracks — that is, the complete trace, point by point —, your photos, your titles and descriptions, your posts, comments, reviews and event attendance, and your trail reports: their type, their position, the track you were following if any, and their date.

Your connections in the app: the people you follow, the posts you like, the tracks you mark as favourites, and the list of people you have blocked.

Your ride groups: the group (its code, who created it, its dates, the chosen track if there is one), the list of its members with the date they joined, and, for one hour, your code attempts.

Your downloads: which tracks you downloaded, reworked in "My version" or borrowed in the editor ("Borrow a track"), and when. They are used to apply the monthly quotas and to show how many riders took a track in the last 30 days — a total, never names.

Your use of the track editor: the number of route calculations you request each day, so that a single account cannot exhaust the service. Never the points themselves.

Your messages: your private messages and those you write in an event chat, kept on our servers so they can be delivered to their recipients.

Your reports: the reason you pick, the comment you write, and the decision taken on them.

Your feedback: the message you send us from the app, help request or suggestion, and the email address you give for the reply.

Your notifications: if you allow them, a technical identifier for your phone, needed to deliver them to it, and the types of notifications you chose to receive.

Your Premium subscription, if you take one: the plan, its dates, whether it renews, the store where you took it, your country and the price paid. This information comes from the store and is kept by RevenueCat (section 6); our database keeps only the fact that you are a subscriber and the end date of your period. We never see your payment method.

Moderation decisions concerning you: for a suspension or closure of your account, the decision, its duration, whether it follows a report, the facts held against you, the section of the Terms of Use concerned, the team member who took it and the date; for removed content, the same information appears in the notification sent to you.

Technical data: your IP address and device information, sent automatically with every network connection, plus connection logs kept by our host. And the time of your last activity in the app, used only to count the people online right now: a total, seen by administrators only.

We do not ask for your name, postal address, phone number or any payment details.

3. Why, and on what legal basis

Running the service — your account, the map, the feed, messages, events, the vehicle screen, the Premium subscription and its reminders before renewal: this is performance of the contract between us, namely the Terms of Use you accepted.

Moderating and protecting the service — handling reports, enabling blocking, removing content, suspending or closing an account and keeping a record of those decisions, limiting the number of route calculations per account and ride group code attempts: our legitimate interest in keeping the place usable, and other users' interest in not being abused.

Giving you the reasons for a moderation decision concerning you, telling the person who reported content what we decided, and alerting the authorities if content gives rise to a suspicion of a criminal offence threatening someone's life or safety: this is a legal obligation (EU Digital Services Act).

Showing your position on the map, following it during navigation — including with the screen off —, suggesting nearby tracks on the vehicle screen, and sharing it live during an event or in a ride group: your consent, given when you allow location access and, for live sharing, when you switch it on explicitly or accept it at the start of the navigation. You can withdraw it at any time.

Sending you notifications: also your consent, given when you allow them after the app asks. You can withdraw it at any time in your phone's settings, or choose type by type what you receive in Settings > Notifications. Subscription reminders, however, fall under the contract: they are shown in the app and, if you have allowed notifications, also sent as notifications.

Answering your help requests and suggestions: our legitimate interest in providing support.

Responding to an authority that requires it: our legal obligation.

As of today, we do not sell any data and we earn no advertising revenue. Should that change, section 7 says on what conditions.

4. Location, plainly

This is the most sensitive point in a track-sharing app, so let us be precise.

Your live position is used to place you on the map. It is only written to our databases if you choose to: for a trail report, placed where you are, or for a recorded ride you decide to keep. It also leaves your phone, without being stored anywhere, in two cases: live sharing, described here, and the "Near me" list on the vehicle screen, described below. If you turn on position sharing during an event, or accept it at the start of a navigation in a ride group, your position is broadcast live to the other participants of that event or to the members of that group. That sharing is ephemeral, written to no table, and stops as soon as you leave the screen — or, if you are navigating, at the end of the navigation, even if your screen has turned off in the meantime. Participants also see your distance along the trace and whether you have stopped; the group is alerted if you stay stopped for more than 5 minutes or fall more than 3 km behind. You can also send them a quick message picked from a short list ("Break", "Fuel", "Wait for me"…): it appears next to your username for 3 minutes for those sharing their position, without a notification, then disappears; it is never stored. Members following the navigation on a vehicle screen also see your position and username there.

A ride group is joined with a 6-character code, valid for 24 hours. When you share that code, it goes through the app you choose — WhatsApp, SMS… —, under its own rules. Only give it to people you ride with: anyone who has the code can join the group, up to 30 members. You are asked whether to share your position at every start, and you can leave the group at any time. Someone you have blocked, or who has blocked you, cannot join a group you belong to, and blocking a member removes them from the group.

On a vehicle screen, with Apple CarPlay or Android Auto, the display goes through Apple or Google, on your phone and your vehicle, under their own rules. To do this, ADVMAP hands them, on your phone, what is to be displayed: the map, your position, the track being followed (its name and its end point), the track-following indications (next turn, distance and arrival time, surface, points of attention, fuel stations), the position and username of the members of your riding group if you ride in one, and the list of tracks you open on the vehicle screen. Group members can be hidden in the vehicle screen settings ("Riding group"). We send them neither your email address nor any other data from your account. ADVMAP reads no vehicle data (speed, fuel, mileage) and does not use the microphone. A navigation started from the vehicle screen does not share your position with a ride group or an event. To suggest nearby tracks ("Near me"), the app sends our server your position rounded to about 1 km: it returns the closest public tracks, without keeping your position or writing it to any table. Your vehicle screen settings and your acceptance of its safety warning stay on your phone.

If you let ADVMAP record your ride during navigation, the recording stays on your phone: it is only sent if you choose to keep it, as a private track, or to publish it.

When you download a map for offline use — a whole region, or an area around a track or around the map displayed —, it is kept on your phone, together with the track itself if you downloaded it from a track, until you delete it, in Settings > Offline maps. To suggest the regions around you, the app uses your last known position, on your phone only: it is sent nowhere. If you sign out, the track is erased and the area loses its name; a track that is no longer available — deleted, made private — is erased from your phone the next time you are online.

A trail report is placed where you are when you make it. Others see it without ever knowing who made it.

During a navigation you started, ADVMAP keeps following your position and making its voice announcements when the screen turns off or the app goes into the background. Your phone shows it — a blue indicator on iPhone, a persistent notification on Android — and everything stops at the end of the navigation. ADVMAP never asks for access to your location "always", only while using the app, and stores nothing more than with the screen on. Voice announcements are produced by your phone, without a microphone.

Your GPX tracks contain precise coordinates by nature. When you publish a track publicly, the trace is visible to everyone, including its starting point — which is often where you actually set off from. Bear that in mind before publishing a track that starts outside your front door.

The file other people download is a cleaned copy of yours: without the times you passed each point, without any author details it may contain, and without its named points ("Home", "Bivouac"…), unless you choose to show them. Likewise, your photos are re-saved before upload: the hidden information they contain — where and when they were taken, the camera model — is removed. A photo that cannot be cleaned is not uploaded.

When you import a track, save a track from the editor or keep a recorded ride, even as private, the coordinates of its starting point are sent to an outside geocoding service to find the country, region and town shown on the track page. The same applies when you pick a place for a post, an event or "My area".

When terrain analysis runs on a track, the complete trace is sent to outside mapping services to work out which sections are paved, and to find points of attention (fords, barriers…) and useful places (fuel stations, campsites…) along the trace. Section 6 says to whom. The elevation of an imported track that has none is worked out on your phone, from relief data downloaded for the area it crosses.

In the track editor, the points you place on the map stay on your phone, as a draft, until you save the track. You can keep up to 10 drafts, in Create > "My drafts", where you can delete them; beyond that, the oldest is erased, and all of them are erased from this phone when you sign out or your session ends. To calculate the route between them, they are sent to Geoapify through our server, without your name or IP address. "Check the route" also sends the drawn route, before it is saved, to the analysis services in section 6, to find points of attention and, for Premium subscribers, useful places. For an "Automatic loop", your starting point and the waypoints calculated around it are sent to Geoapify in the same way.

Finally, whenever you choose to open a point in a navigation app — for example with "Directions to the start", "Rejoin the route", "Head to the start" during a navigation, or "Directions to here" after a long press on the map —, the coordinates of that point are passed to the app you select (Google Maps, Waze or Maps), which then applies its own privacy rules.

5. Who sees what

What you publish publicly — public tracks, posts, comments, reviews — is visible to other users. Your profile (username, picture, bio, motorcycle, and the badges shown next to your username, including the Premium badge while your subscription is active and the Admin badge of team members) is visible to everyone, even without an account; the "Profile search" setting only decides whether people can find you by searching for your username. Your area, your last activity, your favourites and your settings are visible only to you.

In a ride group, the other members see your username and profile picture; your position, your distance along the track and your quick messages only during a navigation in which you agreed to share them.

Your private messages are read by nobody but their recipient in the normal running of the service; those in an event chat, by its participants.

The list of people you block is visible to you alone. The blocked person is not told.

The number of riders who took a track in the last 30 days is shown as a total, never with names.

When you publish a public track or event, the people who follow you may be notified, with its title. Never for a private event, and never between blocked accounts.

Trail reports are only visible to signed-in users, for 30 days, on tracks passing nearby: never on the general map, never with your username, and never between blocked accounts.

One exception, which we would rather write down plainly: when content is reported, an administrator may view it in order to decide whether it should be removed, including where that content is not public. For a message, they see only the reported message — never the rest of the conversation. For a trail report, they see its author's username. That is the price of moderation that actually works. A moderation decision concerning you is visible only to you and the ADVMAP team; if it follows a report, the person who made the report only learns whether the content was removed or action was taken, without the facts or the section of the terms.

Outside those cases — handling a report, answering a help request you send us, or complying with a legal obligation — we will not go looking at your data.

6. Outside services

Running ADVMAP means calling services we do not host ourselves. Here they are, and what each one receives.

Supabase — database, authentication, file storage and live messaging: receives all of your account and content data.

Google and Apple — signing in to your account: receive the sign-in request and pass us your email address. If you allow notifications, they are also the ones who deliver them to your phone. If you subscribe, they are the ones who sell and charge for the Premium subscription, under their own terms and their own privacy policy. If you use the vehicle screen, the display goes through Apple CarPlay or Android Auto: ADVMAP hands them, on your phone, what is to be displayed (map, position, track being followed and track-following indications; in a riding group, the position and username of the other members), and nothing else from your account. Finally, Apple hosts our advmap@sisilab.app mailbox (iCloud): the emails you send us and our replies, including a moderation decision sent by email, go through its servers.

Expo (EAS) — delivering app updates and sending notifications: receives your IP address and device information when an update is downloaded. For a notification, it receives your phone's technical identifier and the text displayed — the username of the person concerned, the action, for example "sent you a message", and, where relevant, the title of the track or event concerned —, then hands it to Apple or Google. Never the content of a message or a comment.

Cloudflare — hosting of our own maps (Road and Off-road base maps, Tracks and paths layer, map fonts, icons and styles, offline regions), on your phone and on the vehicle screen: receives your IP address and the map area you are looking at, or the region you download. These maps are stored in the European Union, and we keep no log of these requests.

GitHub (Microsoft) — hosting of our web pages (terms of use, privacy policy, legal notice and the ADVMAP website), which the app opens in your browser: receives your IP address when you view them.

Esri, IGN and OpenTopoMap — satellite, French mapping and topographic base maps: receive your IP address and the map area you are looking at. When you download an area around a track or around the map displayed, it is supplied by IGN in France, by the official Spanish and Austrian maps in those two countries, and by OpenTopoMap elsewhere or if IGN does not respond: they therefore receive the downloaded area.

The official maps of neighbouring countries — swisstopo (Switzerland), Spain's National Geographic Institute, BKG (Germany), basemap.at (Austria) and Kartverket (Norway) — replace the topographic map in their country: each receives your IP address and the map area you are looking at, never any data from your account.

Amazon Web Services — elevation data, for the map's relief, climb calculations and the elevation of tracks that have none, worked out on your phone: receives your IP address and the area concerned, either the map displayed or the area the track crosses.

Photon (Komoot) — place search and reverse geocoding: receives what you type into the search box, and the precise coordinates of the points to identify.

Geoapify (KEPTAGO LTD, Cyprus) — terrain analysis, points of attention, useful places and route calculation in the track editor: receives the points of the track concerned, or of the route you are preparing in the editor. The request goes through our server: Geoapify receives neither your name, nor your IP address, nor any of your account data. According to its policy, a successful request is normally kept no longer than 24 hours.

Overpass (OpenStreetMap project) — points of attention, plus mountain passes and unstaffed huts among the useful places: receives your IP address, the ways the track follows and the rectangles around it.

RevenueCat — Premium subscription management: receives, directly from your phone, your IP address and technical information (operating system, app version, language), and, from the store, your subscription information (plan, dates, renewal, country and price paid). Your internal identifier is only passed to it after you have accepted the Terms of Use, and only when necessary: when the Premium screen is opened, during a purchase or a purchase restore, or if a Premium subscription taken out in the store is already active on your phone; when you sign out, the link is cut. It receives neither your name, nor your email address, nor any advertising identifier, and never sees your payment method, which stays with Apple or Google.

Versions of the app installed before 23 November 2026, as long as they have not been updated from the store, also call OpenFreeMap for their base maps, OpenMapTiles and unpkg for fonts and the mapping library, cdnjs (Cloudflare) for the PDF roadbook viewer and RainViewer for the weather layer: each receives your IP address and, for OpenFreeMap and RainViewer, the map area you are looking at. Update the app so that it no longer calls them.

None of these services receives your private messages, apart from Supabase, which hosts them.

As of today, ADVMAP contains no analytics tool, no advertising network and no third-party tracker. This is not a statement of intent: there are none in the version you are using, and that can be verified. The next section says what happens if that changes.

7. Advertising

As of today, ADVMAP shows no advertising and contains no ad network.

That may change: the app has to pay for itself, and advertising is one of the options being considered alongside the Premium subscription. Better to say so now than to let you discover it one morning.

If that day comes, here are the ground rules.

We will tell you in the app before the first advert appears, and this page will be updated, with a new date and the names of the ad networks involved.

Advertising that is merely displayed — the same for everyone, without tracking what you do — relies on no data about you and does not require your consent.

Targeted advertising, on the other hand — based on tracking your activity, on an advertising identifier, or on a profile built from your data — will only be switched on with your explicit consent. You will be asked beforehand, you will be able to refuse without losing access to the app, and to withdraw it afterwards whenever you want. That is what the law requires, and we have no intention of getting around it.

In every case, your tracks, your photos and your private messages will not be passed to an advertiser, and your data will not be sold.

8. Transfers outside the European Union

Some of these services are established outside the European Union: Supabase in Singapore, and in the United States Expo, Esri, Amazon Web Services, Cloudflare, GitHub and RevenueCat, as well as unpkg for older versions of the app. Google and Apple deal with European users through their Irish subsidiaries, but may transfer data outside the Union. These transfers rely on the safeguards provided for in Chapter V of the GDPR: mainly the European Commission's standard contractual clauses and, for the US providers that adhere to it, the EU–US Data Privacy Framework.

The OpenStreetMap-world services we use — Photon, Overpass and OpenTopoMap — are hosted in Europe.

Geoapify, based in Cyprus, is a European Union company whose servers are in the Union. We use its European address, which guarantees that our requests are processed only within the Union, under a processing agreement compliant with Article 28 of the GDPR. Our own maps are stored by Cloudflare in the European Union; Cloudflare may route requests through its global network, under the safeguards mentioned above. OpenFreeMap, called only by older versions of the app, is based in Hungary.

The official maps of neighbouring countries come from European public bodies: those of the European Union, of Norway, a member of the European Economic Area, and of Switzerland, which the European Commission recognises as ensuring adequate data protection.

Your data and your files are physically hosted in the European Union, in France: Supabase stores them in the Europe (Paris) region. The Supabase company is Singaporean, but your data is stored in France. Our server functions, which relay some requests (Geoapify, notifications, subscriptions, account deletion), run on Supabase's network, as close as possible to the device calling them, sometimes outside the Union: they read and write your data in the Paris database and keep no copy of it elsewhere. The services listed above receive what is stated for each of them, where they are established.

9. How long we keep your data

For as long as your account exists. An account that has never accepted the Terms of Use is deleted 30 days after it was created.

You can delete a track, a post, an event or a comment at any time: deletion is immediate, and also erases the files attached to it — GPX file, photos. Photos that others attached to their reviews of your track are erased within 48 hours. When you delete a track, or a public track becomes private again, the notifications that announced its publication to the members who follow you are erased; those about reviews of that track are also erased when you delete it.

Your download history is erased automatically after 60 days. Your trail reports stay visible for 30 days, then are permanently erased 7 days later; you can delete them sooner, in Settings > Your data > "My reports". The track editor's route calculation counter is erased after 7 days. A ride group expires 24 hours after it is created and is erased at the latest the following night, together with its member list; your code attempts are only used for one hour and are erased at the latest the following night. Your feedback is erased at the latest one year after you send it, and a report one year after it has been handled. A suspension of your account is kept for as long as it applies, then erased one year after it ends or is lifted. An account closed permanently is deleted one year after its closure, together with the decision, as if you had deleted it yourself; its files are erased within the following 48 hours.

Deleting your account (Settings > Your data) erases your profile and profile picture, your tracks and their GPX files, your photos, your posts, your comments, your reviews, the events you created, the list of members you follow, your likes, your favourites, your block list, your download history, your notifications, your track editor counter, your ride groups, your feedback, your trail reports, your messages and the moderation decisions concerning you. For an account created with Apple, deletion also removes ADVMAP from "Sign in with Apple" when you confirm it with Apple on your iPhone or iPad; otherwise — and always for a closed account deleted automatically —, you can remove it yourself in your Apple account settings, under "Sign in with Apple". We also ask RevenueCat to erase your identifier and the subscription history linked to it; Apple and Google keep their own purchase records, under their own rules. Deleting your account does not stop a Premium subscription: cancel it in your store first. Content reports you made are kept without your name, to protect other users: until they are handled, then one year. It is immediate and final: we cannot restore anything afterwards. If erasing your files fails, your account is not deleted and you can try again: deletion resumes where it stopped. Our host's technical backups may keep a copy for a few days before being overwritten in turn.

Your private conversations disappear for both people: the messages you sent as well as those you received. The other way round, if someone you talk to deletes their account, your conversation disappears on your side too. Only what was copied or screenshotted escapes deletion.

10. Your rights

The GDPR gives you the right to access your data, have it corrected, have it erased, receive it in a readable format, object to certain processing and ask for it to be restricted.

Two of those rights are self-service in the app, with no need to write to us: exporting your data and deleting your account, in Settings > Your data. If your account is suspended or closed, you can no longer get there: write to us, and we will do it for you. The export contains your photos and GPX files as links, valid for 7 days: download them before then.

For everything else, write to advmap@sisilab.app. We reply within one month.

You can also tell us what should happen to your data after your death — erase it, or pass it on to a person you designate — by writing to us at advmap@sisilab.app. Without instructions from you, your heirs may ask us to delete your account.

If you believe we are not respecting your rights, you can complain to the French data protection authority, the CNIL (3 place de Fontenoy, TSA 80715, 75334 Paris Cedex 07, www.cnil.fr). If you live in another country of the European Union or the European Economic Area, you can complain to your own country's data protection authority; in Switzerland, to the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (FDPIC).

11. Security

Traffic between the app and our servers is encrypted. Access to your data is controlled at database level by rules that stop a user reading what is not theirs, and track files are kept in private storage.

No system is infallible. In the event of a data breach likely to create a high risk for you, we will inform you and notify the CNIL, as the law requires.

12. Minimum age

ADVMAP is for people aged 18 or over. We do not knowingly collect data about minors. If we learn that an account was created by someone under 18, it is deleted.
